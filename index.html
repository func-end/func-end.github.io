ZWO Seestar S50 Pro – Tilt Problem

Two units, measured corner by corner

M31 mosaic · 349 × 10 s · LP filter · unit 1 · 7 Oct 2026

I measured star sizes across the frame on two Seestar S50 Pro smart telescopes, the one I bought and its replacement, using hundreds of saved 10-second exposures and ASTAP's star detection. Both had one side of the field that was repeatably softer than the other. On the first unit, which side was soft depended on focus. On the replacement it was the bottom-right corner in every frame.

Unit 1 · bought September 2026

9.7–52%
  • Only two good readings: 9.7% (7 Oct, Alt-Az) and 9.4% (4 Oct, equatorial mode). Both were wherever autofocus happened to land, most likely lucky focus.
  • 32–42% about 20 focus steps either side of autofocus, with the soft side swapping; about 52% at 40 steps.
  • 41–49% on 2 Oct.

Passed my screen only when autofocus happened to land in the right place; failed it 20 steps either side.

Unit 2 · replacement, November 2026

44–47.5%
  • Every repeated run on the first night, with IRCUT and LP filters.
  • Bottom-right was the softest corner in 179 of 179 frames.
  • ASTAP's own tilt inspector read 40–50% on sample frames.

Fails my screen at autofocus.

How the number is worked out

Each saved FITS frame goes through ASTAP's command-line star detection. ASTAP reports every star's half-flux diameter (HFD): the width of the circle that holds half the star's light. A softer star has a larger HFD.

The frame is split into a 3×3 grid, and the median HFD is taken in each corner, using ASTAP's labels: 1A top-left, 1C top-right, 3A bottom-left and 3C bottom-right. For each frame:

corner contrast = (largest corner median HFD − smallest corner median HFD) ÷ median HFD of all stars × 100

A run's figure is the median over its frames. ASTAP's own Image Inspection tilt figure is calculated a little differently. On six sample frames it ranked them in the same order as this method and agreed within 1.5 percentage points on five of them, and within 4.3 on the sixth.

Target
NGC 869, the western half of the Double Cluster: a dense, even star field.

10 s exposures, gain 200, IRCUT filter unless noted, every frame saved. Alt-Az mode unless noted, on a levelled tripod.
Screen
Pass: 15% or less at autofocus, and under 25% at 20 focus steps either side. Worth raising with the seller: 25% or more, repeatably. These thresholds are mine, not ZWO's tolerances.
Why ±20

Unit 1: the soft side followed focus

On 2 Oct the bottom of the frame was clearly softer, and refocusing didn't change that. About a quarter of the field had stars at least 20% wider than the centre.

On 7 Oct I stepped focus by hand around the autofocus position and measured each run.

The 1040 row pools three capture starts taken in twilight. At that fixed focus the bottom row sharpened from 5.8 to 4.7 px over about six minutes, so it also includes the scope settling.

The top and bottom of the frame came to their best focus about 32–35 steps apart. That figure comes from a quadratic fit, and it held across four ways of grouping runs and choosing stars. So the surface of best focus is tilted relative to the sensor. These measurements don't show why: a fixed misalignment, sag or something else could all do this.

It also explains why single nights disagreed. With focus at 1042 the field balanced and measured 9.7%; 20 steps either side gave 32–42%. 4 Oct was my only test in equatorial mode. With autofocus at 1061, NGC 869 measured 9.4% (10th–90th percentile 6.2–12.6%) and NGC 7000 measured 7.0%. The bottom row was still the softest part of the frame in all 56 NGC 869 frames, but the difference was about a quarter of what the Alt-Az nights showed. One night can't show whether mount mode matters.

Both good readings came from autofocus, not from a setting I chose. Autofocus doesn't aim for the balance point, so I read them as lucky focus rather than how the unit normally behaved. On 2 Oct, for comparison, it read 41–49%.

A manual-focus option

Because unit 1's soft side swapped with focus, there was a position in between where the two sides balanced. From the 7 Oct runs it sits at about 1045–1047, between the top row's best focus (about 1061) and the bottom row's (about 1029). Autofocus happened to land close to it that night, by luck: 1042, which measured 9.7%.

The Seestar app lets you turn autofocus off and set focus by hand. So on a unit like this one, setting focus by hand near the balance point looks like a workaround on nights when autofocus lands somewhere else. Focus numbers drift with temperature, so the point would have to be found on the night.

This is untested: none of the hand-set runs was at the balance point. It would also balance the softness rather than remove it, because the top and bottom of the frame would each still be about 16 steps from their own best focus.

Unit 2: bottom-right, every frame

The replacement's first night was 11 Nov, on the same target with the same settings. The first run read 22.4% while the scope was still adjusting to the outside temperature; it wasn't repeated, so it isn't counted. Every run after that read 44–47.5%, including one taken after about two hours outside. Bottom-right was the softest corner in all 179 saved NGC 869 frames, with both filters.

Every run was at an autofocus position, from 1049 to 1060, and all were soft at the bottom right. No runs were taken 20 steps either side, so whether this unit had a balance point that manual focus could reach is unknown.

What a retailer has said

On 2 Nov the German review site blickohnegrenzen.de added an official statement on sensor tilt from Astroshop, a German retailer. According to Astroshop:

  • only units from the first shipment in September are affected;
  • the cause was a faulty plastic part, which ZWO has replaced with a sturdier metal holder;
  • units shipping now come from the improved production.

The statement doesn't name the part or say how to tell an old unit from a new one. It comes from a retailer, and I haven't found a statement from ZWO itself.

My units fit it only partly. Unit 1 was delivered in late September, from the first shipment. Unit 2 shipped on 9 Nov, a week after the statement, and still measured 44–47%. That could mean the seller's stock was older, that the metal holder doesn't fix every unit, or that something else causes the softness on these units. My measurements can't tell which.

Checking your own

  1. Pick a rich star field high in the sky. NGC 869 works well in autumn.
  2. Shoot 10-second exposures with saving of individual frames turned on in the Seestar app.
  3. Leave the scope outside for a while first, and compare repeated runs. A first run can read quite differently from later ones.
  4. Copy the FITS frames to a computer and open them in ASTAP (free). Its Image Inspection tool reports a tilt figure and shows which corner is soft.
  5. If autofocus looks fine, also shoot a few minutes 20 steps above and below it.
  6. Judge on several frames and at least two nights. One frame isn't a verdict.

The measuring tools I used, and an iPad app that gives a quick reading in the field, are free on GitHub: func-end/s50-tilt-tools (MIT licence).

What this does and doesn't show

  • Two units, one owner, one site and a handful of nights.
  • The softness pattern repeated within each unit. Its physical cause isn't established.
  • The corner contrast uses ASTAP's star sizes with my own corner formula. It's close to ASTAP's tilt figure, but not identical.
  • The pass and fail thresholds are my screening rules, not ZWO's specification.
  • Focus, temperature and seeing all move the number, so compare repeated runs rather than single frames.
  • Both units went back to the seller.
