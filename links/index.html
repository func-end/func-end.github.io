The 10 most-visited astronomy websites

These are the ten most-visited astronomy websites, in order of Similarweb's global traffic rank for October 2026. Between them they cover space and astronomy news, what's in the night sky tonight, light pollution maps, an astrophotography gallery and amateur astronomy forums.

I left out shops, telescope and app makers, general-interest sites and space-weather sites.

  1. NASAnasa.govMissions, images and science news from the US space agency.
  2. Space.comspace.comDaily space and astronomy news, with night-sky and telescope guides.
  3. ESAesa.intMissions, images and science from the European Space Agency.
  4. TheSkyLivetheskylive.comWhere the planets, comets and Moon are tonight, with rise and set times for your location.
  5. Cloudy Nightscloudynights.comDiscussion forums for amateur astronomers and astrophotographers.
  6. Light Pollution Maplightpollutionmap.infoHow bright the night sky is where you are, and where to find darker skies.
  7. AstroBinastrobin.comAn astrophotography gallery that shows the equipment and settings behind each image.
  8. Universe Todayuniversetoday.comSpace and astronomy news.
  9. Stellarium Webstellarium-web.orgA planetarium in your browser: the sky from any place, at any time.
  10. BBC Sky at Night Magazineskyatnightmagazine.comObserving guides, how-tos and equipment reviews from the UK astronomy magazine.

My favourite free astronomy tools and guides

These aren't in the top ten, but they're favourites of mine and all free: a telescope field-of-view calculator, astrophotography and planetary imaging software, a guide to telescope and camera threads, and introductory astronomy course notes.

  • Field of View Calculatorastronomy.toolsPick a target and a telescope with an eyepiece, camera or binoculars, and it shows how the target will fit in the view.
  • AstroWizardastrowizard.lukomatico.comFree app for Windows, Mac and Linux that turns a stacked image from a Seestar or any telescope into a finished picture in nine guided steps.
  • PIPP, Planetary Imaging PreProcessorastrowhat.comFree Windows program that crops each frame of a planetary video and keeps only the best ones before stacking. Astro What? hosts it now that the original site is offline.
  • Astronomy Threads Explainedagenaastro.comWhich thread fits which, for eyepieces, filters, cameras and telescopes. The link opens at the webcam-camera thread (M12×0.5).
  • Astronomy Notesastronomynotes.comNick Strobel's introductory astronomy course notes, free to read online, chapter by chapter.
